Do You Need an RCD for Outdoor Lighting in Australia?

Written by Chris Bond, a lighting professional with 18 years of industry experience

Short answer: yes. Since AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amendment 2, all outdoor lighting circuits in Australia require 30 mA RCD (residual current device) protection. This closed a gap in the earlier standard, where some permanently wired outdoor lighting circuits were exempt from RCD protection — that exemption no longer applies.

How the protection can be delivered

RCD protection for an outdoor lighting circuit can come from either:

  • An RCD-protected circuit breaker (RCBO) at the main switchboard, protecting the whole circuit, or
  • RCD-protected outlets at the point of use, where the circuit is fed from switched socket outlets rather than being permanently wired from the board

Type A, not Type AC

Since May 2023, Type AC RCDs are no longer permitted for new or altered electrical installations in Australia — the minimum requirement is now Type A. Type A RCDs detect both AC and pulsating DC residual currents, which matters increasingly as more electronic equipment (including many LED drivers) is connected to circuits. If a circuit is being newly installed or altered, this is the current baseline, not the older Type AC standard some existing installations may still have.

What this actually means if you're getting garden lighting installed This isn't a DIY checklist. The practical takeaway for a homeowner is simple: ask your electrician to confirm the circuit is RCD-protected to the current standard (30 mA, Type A minimum) before the job is signed off. A licensed electrician doing compliant work will already be building to this — it's worth asking to be sure, not because it's commonly missed, but because it's a reasonable and easy thing to confirm.
