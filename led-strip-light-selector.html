📖 Understanding LED Strip Types

SMD Strip — The Workhorse

SMD (Surface Mounted Diode) strips are the most common and widely available. They use individual LED chips spaced along the strip. Higher chip density = smoother light. Available in IP20 (indoor), IP65 (splash), IP67 and IP68 (fully waterproof). Proven, repairable, cost-effective.

COB Strip — Dot-Free, But Newer

COB (Chip on Board) strips use a continuous phosphor coating over closely packed chips — completely eliminating the "dotty" appearance. Excellent aesthetics. However: no independent LM80 lifespan data exists yet, colour consistency can vary between rolls, and heat management requires an aluminium profile. Best mounted in channel.

Neon Silicone Flex

A standard SMD strip encased in a continuous silicone extrusion that diffuses the light and gives a smooth neon-like glow. No dots. IP67 as standard — excellent for outdoor, facades, curved signage, pool surrounds and anywhere a visible, weather-resistant strip is needed. Choose silicone (not PU) — UV stable and rated to -40°C.

Channel + Diffuser — The Dot Fix

An aluminium extrusion profile with a frosted polycarbonate diffuser cover mounted over any standard SMD strip is the most proven solution for eliminating visible dots. The aluminium also acts as a heatsink, extending strip life. Available in surface, recessed, corner and pendant profiles. The right choice for virtually any visible installation.

Understanding CRI

CRI (Colour Rendering Index) measures how accurately colours appear under the light source compared to natural sunlight. It ranges from 0–100, with 100 being perfect.

  • CRI 80 — standard, adequate for general ambient lighting
  • CRI 90+ — high CRI, colours appear vivid and accurate — recommended for kitchens, retail, art and anywhere colour appearance matters
  • CRI 95+ — museum quality, used in art galleries, jewellery display, medical lighting

Never use low-CRI strips for food display, retail, or artwork — colours will look washed out and unnatural. High-CRI strips cost slightly more but the difference in light quality is immediately visible.

Do You Need an Aluminium Channel?

Aluminium extrusion channels serve two functions: heat management and light diffusion. For strips above 10W/m, a channel is strongly recommended — it acts as a heatsink, keeping LED junction temperatures lower and dramatically extending strip lifespan. A strip running hot without a channel may last 10,000–20,000 hours; the same strip in a channel can last 50,000+ hours.

Opal diffuser covers on channels eliminate the visible LED dot pattern, creating a smooth, even glow. This is essential for cove lighting, shelf edge lighting, or anywhere the strip itself is visible to occupants.

Colour Changing — RGB vs RGBW vs Tunable White

  • Single colour — simplest, best light quality, lowest cost. Use when you just want white light.
  • RGB — full colour changing but poor white light quality. Good for entertainment and feature lighting where colour effects are the point.
  • RGBW — adds a dedicated white chip to RGB. Best of both — good colour changing plus quality white. More expensive and requires a compatible 4-channel controller.
  • Tunable White (CCT) — adjusts between warm and cool white. No colour changing, but perfect for human-centric lighting that follows the natural daylight cycle.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between SMD and COB LED strips? ▼
SMD strips have individual LED chips spaced along the strip which can create visible dots without a diffuser. COB strips use a continuous line of tiny chips with a phosphor coating, producing a smooth dot-free line of light. COB is preferred for architectural and cove lighting where a seamless look is important.
What colour temperature should I choose? ▼
2700K–3000K is warm white — ideal for living areas, bedrooms and hospitality. 4000K is neutral white, good for kitchens and retail. 5000K–6500K is cool daylight, suited to workshops and task lighting. For architectural cove lighting, 2700K or 3000K is most common.
What IP rating do I need for bathroom LED strips? ▼
Zone 0 (inside bath/shower) requires IP67 minimum. Zone 1 (above the bath to 2.25m) requires IP44 minimum. Zone 2 (within 0.6m of shower) requires IP44. In practice, IP65 strips are recommended for any bathroom as they handle steam and humidity well.
What is CRI and why does it matter? ▼
CRI (Colour Rendering Index) measures how accurately a light source renders colours compared to sunlight. CRI 80 is standard and adequate for general lighting. CRI 90+ renders colours vividly and accurately — important for kitchens, retail, art and display. Never use low-CRI strips for food display or retail as colours will appear washed out.
Do I need an aluminium channel for my LED strips? ▼
For strips above 10W/m, a channel is strongly recommended — it acts as a heatsink and significantly extends strip lifespan. An opal diffuser cover eliminates visible LED dots, creating a smooth glow. Essential for cove lighting or anywhere the strip itself is visible.
What is the difference between RGB and RGBW strips? ▼
RGB strips mix red, green and blue to create colours but produce poor quality white light. RGBW adds a dedicated white LED chip, giving you both full colour changing and good quality white light from one strip. RGBW requires a 4-channel controller and costs more but is worth it when you want both white and colour capability.
How many lumens do I need for under-cabinet strips? ▼
For task lighting under kitchen cabinets, aim for 400–600 lumens per metre — typically a 9–14W/m strip. For purely decorative ambient lighting, 150–300 lumens per metre is sufficient. Choose CRI 90+ strips for kitchen benchtops to render food colours accurately.
